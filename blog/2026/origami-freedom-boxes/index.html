Origami Freedom Boxes

Created on March 04, 2026

2026   ·   books

Book

The book used with my seniors this week was “Henry’s Freedom Box” by Ellen Levine and Kadir Nelson. The topics addressed in the book include resiliency, injustice, and the fight for freedom. It’s a great choice for leading a group conversation around personal values, sacrifice, and perseverance.

Response Art

The art process includes drawing a symbol of freedom at the center of a 5.5” x8.5” paper (cut a standard printing paper in half or use the full size). For my seniors, I pre-made same sized pages with a bird at the center representing freedom for them to color into. Birds in the book symbolize freedom and hope. Then, fold the paper with the drawing on the inside into a paper box. See this tutorial to learn how to fold your paper easily into a box. Add words or decorations on the outside of your origami freedom box if desired.

Process Questions

  1. What image of freedom came up for you? How do your colors or drawing reflect this?
  2. Did you add anything else around the outside of your freedom box? What was it like making your drawing into a physical box?

Therapeutic Benefits

The overall process promotes meaning-making in the art through symbolic and representational thought. It fosters autonomy, a sense of resiliency, and emotional expression, while also getting in touch with personal values.

Materials

  • 5.5” x 8.5” white printer paper or cardstock
  • Coloring pencils/pens, pencil and eraser
  • Decorative stickers
  • Glitter
